After the competition

We asked our own question of ourselves. It found four holes.

Three of these were not features anyone requested. They are what turned up when the product’s own thesis — the catalog may be lying; do not trust it — was pointed at the product. The fourth is a number we had published for weeks that did not survive being measured honestly.

Three of them turned out not to be about Sidq at all. They are properties of writing a trust signal into a metadata catalog, and they hold for anyone doing it on DataHub — chapter 07 is that, separately.

  • 12 original changes
  • 38 new tests
  • 1,266 tests at review time
  • 84.56% coverage
  • 99 lines of jq
  • Review snapshot: 26 Aug 2026

01 Check the arithmetic

          
Do not trust the arithmetician.

          

            Sidq publishes a policy hash and the complete evidence set with every
            decision, and ENGINE-SPEC.md has always stated how a verdict
            resolves. A specification nobody has implemented twice is a claim, not a
            property — and nothing checked that the shipped engine still did
            what the document said.
          


          
policy      66f48004804c5ce02955699710466b6d58ae7a868f876a4774e548c5c15920b8 (pinned, matches the verdict)
evidence    1 item(s), read from the verdict itself
rules       critical_downstream, wide_blast_radius
re-derived  BLOCK / -
published   BLOCK / -

The published decision follows from the published evidence under the pinned
policy. Re-derived without this project, in 99 lines of jq.


          

            POSIX sh, jq and sha256sum. No Python, no DataHub, no network, and not
            one line of this project. The policy is pinned before anything is
            decided — policy_hash is the sha256 of the raw YAML
            bytes, so a swapped policy is caught rather than quietly producing a
            different answer.

The one divergence, and why the tests are the point rather than the script.

            

              5 > "five" raises TypeError in
              Python and the engine turns that into a blocking
              policy_evaluation_failed. jq orders every pair of values
              totally, so it would have answered where the engine declines
              — and nothing would have shown it until a real catalog produced an
              ill-typed detail. The jq side now refuses in the same places.
            

            

              DECISION.md locks a constant: zero LLM in the
              verdict. That was something you believed because we wrote it
              down. A decision a model participated in cannot be reproduced by
              ninety-nine lines of arithmetic, so now it is something you check.
            

          


          

            Boundary, stated rather than left to be found: this
            re-derives the decision from the evidence, not the evidence from the
            catalog. Gathering evidence still means trusting the gates. The gates are
            mechanical reads; the policy layer is where judgement lives, and that is
            the layer this opens to review.
          

        

      


      

        

          
02 The trust anchor

          
It was living inside the thing we tell you not to trust.

          

            A Sidq receipt was nine structured-property strings on a DataHub dataset,
            and nothing tied one to Sidq. Anyone able to write to the catalog could
            write sidq.verdict = PASS by hand, and every reader accepted
            it. context_hash is no defence: it hashes public catalog data
            with a published function, so a forger computes a valid one.
          


          

            

              
Written by hand, by anyone with catalog access

              
1 · an operator writes PASS by hand
    No signature. Every field is plausible and every field is invented.
    CURRENT RECEIPT · PASS · CONTINUE  urn:li:dataset:(urn:li:dataPlatform:postgres,shop.lookup_country,DEV)
      receipt records PASS; continue
      checked at  2026-08-26T00:00:00Z

      policy      66f48004
      verifier    sidq@0.1.0
      signature   UNATTESTED — no signature to check. Not proven forged; not proven Sidq's

            

            

              
Genuine, signed, unedited — and moved one asset over

              
4 · the genuine signed receipt, pasted onto the PII table
    Nothing is edited. Every field verifies, because every field really was signed — only the asset is different.
    NOT VERIFIED  urn:li:dataset:(urn:li:dataPlatform:postgres,shop.customers_pii,PROD)
      receipt signature does not cover this body; it is not the engine's
      checked at  2026-08-26T00:00:00Z
      commit      c0ffee
      policy      66f48004
      verifier    sidq@0.1.0
      signature   TAMPERED — the signature does not cover this body; do not rely on it

            

          


          

            
The attack that signing the body alone would miss.

            

              The receipt on the right is real. Nothing in it was edited, and every
              field verifies — because every field really was signed. Only the
              asset is different: a PASS lifted off a harmless lookup
              table and pasted onto the one carrying customer data. It fails only
              because the signature covers the asset URN along with the
              body.
            

          


          

            
SIGNED
The signature covers this body, for this URN, under the pinned key.

            
TAMPERED
A signature is present and does not. The judgment is settled before the verdict is even read, so a forged receipt cannot answer may_continue.

            
UNATTESTED
Not proven forged, and not proven ours. A receipt written before a key existed is unproven, not false. Collapsing this into TAMPERED would repeat the mistake the coverage gaps were built to fix: one blank standing for two different facts.

          


          

            Compatibility boundary: signature checks are optional.
            A catalog writer can remove a signature, producing UNATTESTED; that state
            retains the legacy verdict policy. Consumers requiring authenticated
            provenance must require SIGNED as well as an applicable verdict.
            A present invalid signature always requires rechecking.
          

        

      


      

        

          
03 The scars

          
It remembered its successes and forgot its scars.

          

            The auditor already reads the catalog as memory: it fetches the receipts a
            previous run earned and skips those assets, so a bounded budget reaches
            the untouched tail. Coverage gaps — the ERROR
            assertions left on assets a run examined and could establish nothing on
            — are the other half of that memory, and nothing read them back.
          


          
Read back from the catalog, from addresses derived rather than stored:
    2 standing gap(s), in 1 query

Without the scars — every asset looks equally untried:
    1. shop.customers_pii           consequence  90
    2. shop.lookup_country          consequence  50
    3. shop.orders                  consequence  50

With them — consequence still decides, and the gap only breaks ties:
    1. shop.customers_pii           consequence  90  ← a previous run established nothing here
    2. shop.lookup_country          consequence  50
    3. shop.orders                  consequence  50  ← a previous run established nothing here

The PII table resisted a previous run and is still examined first: it is
the most consequential asset in the catalog, and having been hard to
establish is not evidence of being harmless. What the scar settles is the
tie between the two assets at 50 — the budget goes to the one no run has
ever established anything on, because that is where it buys something new.


          

            
It breaks ties, and only ties.

            

              The PII table resisted a previous run and is still examined
              first. Consequence dominates absolutely: resisting a check is not
              evidence of being harmless, and demoting an asset for having been hard
              would bury exactly the ones most worth understanding. What the scar
              settles is the tie between the two assets at 50 — the budget goes
              to the one no run has ever established anything on.
            

          


          

            The addresses are derived, not stored:
            assertion_urn(asset, "not_established") is
            deterministic, so this asks the catalog about addresses it computed rather
            than keeping a list it would also have to keep honest. A transport failure
            yields no gaps rather than inventing scars, and says so on stderr —
            an operator never told would read a degraded order as a decision.
          

          

            The line this does not cross. The agent learns
            where to look. It never learns what to decide. The
            policy stays hand-written, deterministic, and re-derivable by a reader who
            has none of this — which is chapter 01.
          

        

      


      

        

          
04 A chosen number

          
The held-out set was doing three jobs at once.

          

            The documentation reader published 95.8% precision. That
            figure came from a held-out set that had also chosen the head and
            chosen the operating threshold. A number that is the maximum of a search
            over the same rows it is then reported on is an upper bound of that
            search, not a measurement.
          


          
    
            
  • Published before95.8% / 58.0%threshold chosen on the rows it was reported from
    • 
            
  • Selection bias alone95.2% / 49.6%same corpus, honestly chosen threshold
    • 
            
  • Published now97.1% / 55.5%measured once, on 528 rows selection never saw
    • 
          


          

            Three splits: 1,538 rows fit the candidates,
            510 carved out of training — unseen by the fit, so
            a threshold is not chosen where the head is strongest — select the
            head and the threshold, and the figure is measured once on
            the 528 rows selection never touches. Nothing is searched
            there. The chosen head then ships refit on all 2,048: selection being over,
            keeping a quarter of the corpus unused cost 0.7 points of precision and 6.7
            of recall for nothing.
          

          

            The precision claim survived honest measurement and went up. The recall
            claim was the one the same selection had inflated. Against the deterministic
            rule baseline on the same rows — 17.4% precision,
            3.4% recall — the reader is not close.
          

          

            Two smaller things this turned up. catboost was imported at
            module scope and is in no lockfile, so --fit could not run in
            any environment this repository pins: the number was not re-derivable by
            anyone who installed what the locks say. And the split is deterministic
            without a seed — every fourth row within each label goes to
            calibration, so it is the same split on any machine and no label can be
            missing from it by luck.
          

        

      


      

        

          
05 What we did not do

          
99% precision, measured rather than promised.

          

            It was asked for, and it was attempted rather than dismissed. Every row
            below is a real run: the target was raised, the head was swept across
            regularisation strengths and a neural alternative, and each winner was
            measured once on the untouched rows.
          


          

            
target 0.95
97.06% precision · 55.46% recall · 68 proposals · 95% CI [0.899, 0.992]

            
target 0.99
97.67% precision · 35.29% recall · 43 proposals · 95% CI [0.879, 0.996]

            
neural head
98.28% precision · 47.90% recall · 58 proposals · 95% CI [0.909, 0.997]

          


          

            
At this corpus size, 99% is not distinguishable from 97%.

            

              Every interval above overlaps almost completely. Raising the target buys
              0.6 points of precision and costs 20 points of recall; the neural head
              buys 1.2 and costs 7.6. Trading measurable recall for
              unmeasurable precision is not an improvement, and publishing
              99% would be claiming a resolution the data cannot support — which
              is the exact error chapter 04 removes.

            Recall has not flattened. The binding constraint is labelled data,
            not the model, and no amount of tuning substitutes for it.
          

          

            Also unfinished and worth naming: the checks on the four public demo pull
            requests are red. One workflow asks for issues: write where
            commenting on a pull request is governed by pull-requests, and
            one graph recording was missing from the committed fixtures so a gate
            crashed instead of refusing to certify. Both fixes are included in the post-competition code. The historical
            pull-request checks remain records of the revisions they tested.
          

        

      


      

        

          
07 For DataHub

          
Three of these are not about Sidq.

          

            Everything above reads as a project auditing itself, and that is a fair
            description of how the work happened. But three of the findings are
            properties of writing a trust signal into a metadata catalog at
            all — they would have surfaced for anyone building this on
            DataHub, and they did not need Sidq to be true.
          


          

            
1 · ERROR already means “examined, could not establish”. Almost nobody uses it.

            

              An assertion result carries SUCCESS,
              FAILURE, INIT and ERROR. Most
              writers use the first two, so the catalog ends up showing the same
              blank for nobody looked and someone looked and could
              establish nothing — two facts a data platform has every
              reason to keep apart.
            

            

              ERROR is the distinction, and it is already in the model.
              A check that could not complete is reported as one, in the Quality tab,
              beside the checks that did. It also self-retires: sharing the rule-id
              namespace means the first later run that establishes something closes
              the gap through the ordinary sweep, with no second mechanism to build
              or keep honest.
            

          


          

            
2 · A structured property is metadata. Metadata does not carry provenance.

            

              Write verified = true, reviewed_by,
              certified or any other trust signal into a property, and a
              reader gets the value with no way to tell your write from anyone
              else’s. Access policy governs who may write; it does not
              let the reader of a value check who did. That is not a defect
              in DataHub — it is what metadata is — but it is a load-bearing
              fact for anyone putting a trust decision there.
            

            

              The fix is small and portable: sign the canonical body together
              with the asset URN, publish the public key with the writer, and
              answer in three states so “unsigned” stays distinct from
              “forged”. Chapter 02 is that, and the URN is in the payload
              because without it a genuine signal moves between assets untouched.
            

          


          

            
3 · A decision in the catalog should carry enough to be recomputed without you.

            

              A verdict written into DataHub is a claim, and a claim a reader cannot
              recompute is one they can only trust. Publishing the policy identity and
              the evidence the decision consumed — not just the outcome —
              turns it into arithmetic anyone can check, in any language, without the
              engine that produced it. Ninety-nine lines of jq was the whole cost here.
            

          


          

            And one that is not a pattern but a measurement: the catalog is a
            sufficient coordination substrate. Four concurrent
            workers with no coordinator, no IPC and one killed mid-run share nothing
            but DataHub — receipts say what has been established, coverage gaps
            say what resisted, and the pair is enough to divide a bounded budget and
            converge across runs. That is in the submitted entry, not here; this work
            only supplied the missing half.
